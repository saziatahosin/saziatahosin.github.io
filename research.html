Research

Trustworthy AI, from hospital scans to satellite images.

High accuracy on a benchmark is not enough. My work asks whether a model can explain itself, protect privacy, and still hold up across different hospitals, scanners, populations and landscapes.

01 / IMAGING

Medical image analysis

Deep learning architectures, including vision transformers (MViTX), gated MLPs (ECgMLP), attention mechanisms and hybrid transfer learning, for diagnosing brain tumours, chest X-ray disease, COVID-19 from CT, endometrial cancer from histopathology, and skin disease.

Vision transformersTransfer learningAttentionMRI · CT · X-ray
02 / TRANSPARENCY

Explainable AI

Pairing every model with explanations, such as saliency maps and feature attributions, that show clinicians which regions or features drove a prediction, so errors can be caught and trust can be earned.

Saliency mapsFeature attributionClinical interpretability
03 / PRIVACY

Privacy-preserving learning

Federated learning (FedVGM) that trains one model across several medical image datasets without pooling patient data, and self-supervised learning that reduces the need for costly expert annotation.

Federated learningSelf-supervisedMulti-dataset
04 / GLOBAL HEALTH

Women’s, maternal & child health

Machine learning for child and maternal mortality risk factors, fetal anaemia and fetal health classification, and breast cancer prediction for Bangladeshi patients, aimed at tools that work where specialist care is scarce.

Risk predictionLow-resource settingsEnsemble learning
05 / CLINICAL PREDICTION

Clinical decision support

Optimised machine-learning pipelines, with feature selection, hyperparameter tuning and ensembles, for heart disease, heart attack (ECG), thyroid and hepatitis C prediction.

Feature selectionEnsemblesTabular clinical data
06 / LANGUAGE

Low-resource language NLP

Transformer and recurrent models for Bangla sentiment analysis and fake-news detection, bringing language AI to under-served languages.

TransformersBiGRUBangla
07 / ENVIRONMENT

Environmental AI: drought from space

I apply deep learning, transfer learning and explainable AI to satellite imagery to detect, map and forecast drought, using the same image-analysis methods as my medical work, with models whose decisions can be checked.

Remote sensingMultispectral imageryTime seriesVegetation indicesExplainable AI

Approach

How I approach a problem

STEP 01

Start from the clinical question

What decision does the model support, who uses it, and what does a mistake cost?

STEP 02

Test across datasets, not just one

Models are evaluated on multiple public datasets to check they generalise beyond a single benchmark.

STEP 03

Explain every prediction

Explainability is built in from the start, not added at the end.

STEP 04

Respect privacy

Where data can’t move, the model moves instead, through federated and self-supervised learning.

STEP 05

Transfer methods across domains

The same image-analysis toolkit that reads a CT scan can read a satellite image. Moving ideas between medicine and environment is where I see new impact.
